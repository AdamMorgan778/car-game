# Three Lanes

The engine wakes with a cough and a hum,
the dashboard glows like a rising sun,
and the road unrolls in a long grey thread
with painted dashes that race ahead.

A red car swerves, a blue one stalls,
a yellow one drifts toward the median walls;
I tap to the left, I slide to the right,
my small white car in the borrowed light.

The faster I go, the more they come,
a chrome-bright flood, a rolling drum,
and I know the crash is waiting there,
a heartbeat off, a hair, a hair.

But oh, the lean of the turning wheel,
the trust of rubber, the hum of steel,
the open stretch where the traffic thins:
that's where every driver wins.

So press the space and start once more;
the road forgets the score before.
There's always one more lane to find,
and all the cars you leave behind.
